## Residential market — now backed by an institutional source

[Knight Frank](https://www.knightfrank.co.uk/research/article/2026/5/marrakeshs-new-momentum) —
a global real estate advisory with an actual research desk, not a brokerage
marketing blog — puts prime Marrakech residential at **€5,500–€7,000 per
square metre**, with price growth estimated at **10–15% over the past two
years** and a further **~6%** forecast for 2026. Prime demand clusters in
**Royal Palm, Amelkis, Palmeraie, and select Medina properties**.
Transactions across all segments rose **12% in 2024**, and the buyer
profile has shifted from mostly retirees toward younger (40–50s), globally
mobile families — European buyers (French, Belgian, British), the Moroccan
diaspora, US-based professionals, and Middle Eastern purchasers. Short-term
rental yields run **7–10%**; most buyers rent for 6–12 months before
purchasing ("rental-first" pattern); international buyers face roughly
**10%** in transaction costs. The report also flags limited turnkey
inventory and uneven construction quality outside branded, hotel-anchored
developments — a concrete supply-side risk for the Real Estate Sector
Agent to track.

This is still one research house's view, not a government statistic —
label it MEDIUM-HIGH credibility (named institutional author, methodology
implied but not published inline), one notch below `macro_morocco.md` and
`tourism_stats.md`, which cite the central bank and Ministry of Tourism
directly.

## Hotel construction pipeline — now with a named report and hard numbers

Morocco has **75 hotels and 10,606 rooms** in its development pipeline —
second in Africa behind Egypt (**185 hotels, 45,984 rooms**), ahead of
Nigeria in third — per the **2026 Hotel Chain Development Pipelines in
Africa** report by **W Hospitality Group**, as covered by
[Hespress](https://en.hespress.com/137054-morocco-ranks-second-among-top-10-african-countries-for-hotel-development-with-75-hotels-10606-rooms.html)
(published 2026-05-02). Morocco's pipeline grew from 7,622 rooms in 2024 to
8,579 in 2025; Casablanca accounts for roughly a third of the current
pipeline (so this figure is a national capacity signal, not a
Marrakech-specific one — worth flagging in any report that cites it
alongside Marrakech-only data). This replaces the earlier Morocco Beat
citation for the same underlying claim with the actual named industry
report it was presumably drawing on.

## Credibility caveat (why this file still isn't a citable conclusion alone)

Even the upgraded sources above are a research house and a trade-press
write-up of an industry report — not official government or
international-institution statistics (contrast with `macro_morocco.md` and
`tourism_stats.md`, which cite Bank Al-Maghrib and Ministry of
Tourism/DEPF data directly). This is a real, live instance of the "is the
source primary or secondary, and is there potential bias" credibility
criterion from the Concept & Architecture Report, not a hypothetical one —
and it is exactly why the Source Credibility Agent (Phase 4/5) needs to
grade sources on a spectrum rather than a binary trust/don't-trust.
